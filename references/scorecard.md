# Model, build, score, attack (the Winning Offer: THE MODEL through ATTACK)

## The model check (first; the bundle column from the research feeds it)

1. **Pick the model from precedent, not preference.** Lay out the operators' splits
   (pure one-time / pure recurring / hybrid) next to what each one actually sells. The
   rule: **the split matches the goods.** A durable asset sells one-time; a living
   service sells recurring; half-and-half sells hybrid. Copy the split of the operator
   whose goods most resemble the founder's, and note which splits in this market are
   cooling (a model everyone is leaving is a warning).
2. **The one-tenth test** (Hormozi's): if this offer had to cost a tenth and deliver
   MORE, what would you invent? Whatever that is, propose pulling it into the standard
   tier AT THE NUMBERS GATE; the founder decides. On the document it enters through the stack
   only after that decision; an undecided find goes in the document's WHAT WEEK ONE MAY ATTACK
   NEXT section, never silently into the stack.
3. **The day-31 attack** (only when there is a recurring fee): the buyer has paid, the
   thing works, the first renewal arrives. Name exactly what VISIBLY BREAKS for them if
   they cancel. For a service business, "breaks" means the service stopping (the grounds
   go unmowed), not a feature switching off; translate accordingly. If the answer is
   "nothing they can feel," the recurring fee has no teeth: move something genuinely
   recurring behind it, or lower it, but never average between. Named case, the audit of
   an existing business with a live but toothless mechanic: the numbers gate forces the
   decision on the spot; either the founder fixes the mechanic now, or the document prints
   the honest gap and week one attacks it. The document never prints a cancel line that
   pretends teeth exist. A split decision at the gate (grandfather existing buyers, new
   terms for new ones) is a valid resolution: print both lines. And when teeth obviously
   exist, still ask one question: "has anyone cancelled and, you suspect, not missed
   it?" A healthy-looking mechanic can be the founder's blind spot too.

One collision warning: everything the model check concludes (the pick, the price
proposal, AND the day-31 cancel-line wording) gets presented together at the numbers
gate. The cancel line is a founder decision, not an agent's afterthought.

## Build through the value equation

Two frameworks stack here, both proven:

**First, the One Big Domino** (Brunson): write the single belief that, if the buyer holds
it, makes buying inevitable and every objection fall. One sentence, starting "If they
believe ___, they buy." The sentence and the promise exist to install exactly that belief;
anything in the copy that does not push the domino gets cut.

**Then the value equation** (Hormozi): dream outcome × belief it will work ÷ time ×
effort. Each dial must become a PHRASE in the copy, not a score on a sheet:

- **Dream outcome**: write it in the buyers' words from the research (their outcome
  words, never yours).
- **THE SENTENCE**: one sentence that says what the business is. Test: a stranger hears
  it once and can repeat it back. If not, rewrite.
- **THE PROMISE**: three sentences max, built from the mined words directly (reuse the
  buyers' phrases, do not paraphrase them). Time commitments only for things the founder
  controls. "Controls" means the deliverable state the founder installs (a system running,
  a plan delivered, a hire made together), never the buyer's own follow-through. If a time
  claim sits near an outcome claim, add the honesty line: "X days to ready, not X days to
  results."
- **Effort**: name the ONE thing the buyer still does. Small, concrete, true.
- **Belief**: one proof phrase a stranger can verify without trusting you.

## The seven gates (no receipt, no score)

1. The buyer is findable in numbers
2. The buyer has money (the only gate that kills instantly)
3. The problem is expensive to keep
4. Competitors are selling (demand proof)
5. An empty seat exists
6. The value equation, no numeric scores: name the STRONGEST dial and the WEAKEST dial,
   each with one receipt. (A 1-10 score with no rubric is an invented number, and this
   skill bans invented numbers.)
7. The number chain (how buyers currently reach them, stage by stage). **One stage is
   already on paper: the objection.** The NOT NOW rows the founder pasted at C0 are it,
   each reason quoted word for word out of column 5, `What they said on the call`. Count
   those rows and read the reasons; that is the whole chain this run holds. Nothing else
   here opens the founder's sheet, so every other stage (sent, replies, calls, money, plus
   views and clicks where a content lane exists) is ASKED for at the numbers gate, in one
   batch, never mid-SCORE. Show the chain back in one line for a yes. The window is
   whichever of these three they can actually answer: the warm sprint just run, the last
   full buying cycle, or trailing twelve months divided by twelve. Never the calendar
   month.
   **Who owns the week-one blank, and this is the only place the rule lives** (the document
   template and the run file point here): the WEAKEST STAGE of the chain, and only a stage
   that EXISTS in this founder's funnel today can take it. Weakest means the biggest
   drop-off between two stages, or a stage that exists and nobody counts, and an uncounted
   stage outranks any counted one. Two or more uncounted, and the EARLIEST takes the
   blank: a downstream stage cannot be fixed by a founder who never fed it. A stage that
   does not exist yet is a different thing from one nobody counts: a founder who sends
   every message by hand has no views and no clicks, so those are absent, not uncounted.
   Name them on the document as not in play, and put standing one up in WHAT WEEK ONE MAY
   ATTACK NEXT, never in the blank. Never the lowest number either: a funnel only
   decreases, so the lowest number is always the last stage, which is the output, never
   the constraint. One thing outranks the whole chain, an unsaid price from the numbers
   gate, because no stage gets fixed by a founder who cannot quote their own number.
   On a warm run no chain is asked: this gate prints `unscored: no call touched it`, and the
   blank goes to an unsaid price first, then to the earliest gate printed unscored.
   Write the chain and its window into `squad/offer-research.md` under `## SCORECARD`,
   next to this gate. Name the stages in the founder's own vocabulary (sent, replies,
   calls, money), and add views and clicks only where a content lane actually exists; say
   plainly which stages do not exist yet. If the funnel is a single step (post → DM → sale), the
   uncounted-stage rule already answers it: the one live stage nobody counts IS the weakest
   stage, and it goes in the blank.

Score each gate with the receipt next to it, appended to `squad/offer-research.md` under
`## SCORECARD`, by that exact heading. A gate without a receipt stays unscored and is said out loud; on a warm run, where the
calls are the only receipts, it prints `unscored: no call touched it`. An unscored gate never fails the offer; it prints on the document as
unmeasured and becomes a week-one attack candidate. Only a receipt showing a gate FAILS
triggers the second answer form.

What each gate can do to the run, since the seven do not behave alike:

- **Gate 2 fails: the run stops there.** No document gets built. A buyer with no money is not
  an offer problem to fix later; say it, and the conversation turns to which buyer does
  have money.
- **Gates 1, 3, 4 and 5 fail: the document still prints**, carrying the second answer form
  ("This offer fails gate ___ because ___") and the fix.
- **Gates 6 and 7 are diagnostic, never pass or fail.** Gate 6 produces the blind spot,
  gate 7 produces the week-one target. Neither is ever named in a fails-gate answer.

## The three self-attacks (run before showing anything)

- **Buyer voice**: does every claim answer a verbatim pain from the research? Cut what
  answers nothing.
- **The shelf**: put the sentence next to the top 10 operators' real pages. If it blends
  in, rewrite. If it copies anyone's words, rewrite.
- **The skeptic**: read it as a burned buyer who has been sold twice before. Every clause
  that smells like hype gets rewritten or gets a receipt.

Write each attack and the fix it forced into `squad/offer-research.md`, appended under
`## ATTACK LOG`, by that exact heading, visibly. An attack with
no written fix (or no written "survived") did not happen. Fix what fails and re-attack
until all three pass. Last check, because BUILD ran before SCORE: re-read THE PROMISE
against gate 6's weakest dial. If the promise claims exactly the thing the weakest dial
lacks, the promise overclaims; rewrite it before the document prints.
